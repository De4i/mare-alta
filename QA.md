# Browser QA

The Playwright harness `browser_qa.py` opens the static game in desktop Chromium at 1280x720 with SwiftShader. It checks that New Game renders the active mission, sends normal walking and sprint input, exercises interaction/fire/reload/damage/wanted controls, completes three story jobs by moving the player to their live objective coordinates and pressing E, starts the courier route, purchases an engine tune in the garage, pauses, saves, reloads and continues, and invokes the death/recovery path. It captures four JPEG frames at the end.

The harness does not establish production-grade combat AI, physical collision, full map traversal balance, or all eight jobs' narrative quality. Its QA waypoint and activity setup place the player at real mission/activity locations; mission, role perk, reward, save and UI handlers remain the game systems. Pursuit escape uses a timed break in line of sight with role-specific decay. At four wanted stars a persistent helicopter — a fuselage, a tail boom and a turning rotor — orbits and shadows the player, and at three stars a physical roadblock is placed on the road ahead of the player. Role attributes now bite: Aim tightens the firing spread and raises damage per hit, Endurance raises maximum health, armour and sprint stamina, every resolved hit paints a short impact flash on the target, and incoming damage briefly tints the screen edge. Any failed browser checks or gate rows are recorded in the build session rather than claimed as passes.

The harness calls `window.setCapturePreset(name)` with `cover`, `onfoot`, `harbour` and `cityrecord`. Each preset advances two fixed simulation ticks before holding for capture. Every required mesh is projected from all eight world-bounds corners; checks fail if it is hidden, behind the camera or outside NDC [-1, 1] on any axis, and the capture is not taken. The broad sea, quay and promenade use explicit surface patches, while boats, masts, the gantry crane and its trolley, container stacks, people and vehicles use full mesh bounds. Beyond per-element bounds, each frame re-counts what lies inside the frustum: the cover asserts two cruisers with their red and blue lamps in the lit phase, at least eight moving vehicles and twelve pedestrians in frame; the harbour asserts moored boats with masts, a gantry crane with its trolley, container stacks, three promenade vehicles and six pedestrians in frame. Additional assertions cover the car's 10% margin, police lamp emissive state, distances, HUD values, minimap job marker, and unchanged measurements while held. This establishes frustum inclusion, not pixel-level visibility or occlusion.

Run `python -u browser_qa.py` against the game served at `http://127.0.0.2:8085/` (8083 was occupied) to reproduce checks and captures.
